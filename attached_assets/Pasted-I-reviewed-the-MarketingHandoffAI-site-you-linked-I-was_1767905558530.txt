Here are the key legal compliance components an SaaS landing page must have to reasonably pass legal review:

1. Terms of Service / Terms of Use
A SaaS business needs clear Terms of Service that govern user access, subscription/licensing, account creation, user obligations, prohibited use, IP rights, disclaimers, limitations of liability, governing law, dispute resolution, etc.

I did not see these clearly linked on the site.

Without these, a legal review would conclude the site is missing required contractual protections for your business.

2. Privacy Policy
Any SaaS site that collects personal data (email, names, analytics cookies, IP addresses, etc.) must publish a compliant privacy policy covering: what data you collect, why, how it’s used, retention, third-party sharing, rights of users (GDPR, CCPA/CPRA, etc.), cookies/consent mechanisms, security practices, etc.

I did not see a Privacy Policy on the marketinghandoffai.com domain.

If you store or process data about EU or California residents, GDPR and CCPA apply; you must have disclosures and lawful bases for processing as required under those laws.

3. Cookie / Tracking Consent
If you use analytics, cookies, pixels, or tracking (common on marketing/SaaS sites), you must have proper consent controls and disclosures to comply with EU cookie laws and many privacy frameworks. 
Onsaas

4. Data Processing Agreements / Subprocessor Disclosure
If your service processes user data through third parties (e.g., analytics, hosting, CRM, email providers), regulations like GDPR require disclosure of subprocessors and obligations via Data Processing Agreements (DPAs). 
Storyteq

5. Intellectual Property & Copyright Notice
You need notice regarding ownership of site content and software, and a statement about permitted use versus prohibited use.

6. Legal Disclaimers / Accuracy of Claims
Any claims about AI capabilities must be accurate and non-misleading. The FTC and other regulators hold marketing content to standards that prohibit deceptive practices; vague or over-broad claims can trigger scrutiny.

7. Accessibility / Compliance Standards
While not always legally mandated depending on jurisdiction, accessibility statements and compliance with standards like WCAG are increasingly expected and enforced.

To pass legal review, you should ensure the site includes at minimum:

A comprehensive Terms of Service agreement that users must agree to before account creation or service use.

A privacy policy that is up to date with GDPR, CCPA/CPRA, and any other relevant privacy laws.

Clear cookie consent mechanisms if tracking is present.

A documented Data Processing Agreement for any third-party data processors.

Intellectual property rights and disclaimers appropriate to your service domain.

Legal review also typically involves a licensed attorney drafting and verifying these documents to match your business model, data practices, and jurisdictions where you operate or have users.
